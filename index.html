I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at Binghamton University (SUNY). I expect to complete my degree in May 2027 and I am on the 2026–27 economics job market.

My research uses microdata and quantitative methods to study firm productivity and the dynamics of firms and industries. I also work on structural econometric modeling, with a focus on the identification of production functions. I examine how technology adoption changes production, input choices, and market power. The central difficulty is that firms choose inputs using information about productivity that researchers do not observe, and my work addresses that endogeneity in order to distinguish technological improvement from other sources of firm performance.

My job market paper, Digitalization, Factor-Biased Productivity, and Market Power, separates labor-augmenting productivity, Hicks-neutral productivity, and markups, which a single productivity residual holds together. Before graduate school I spent close to five years in data science and engineering across banking, e-commerce, media measurement, and workforce development.
