About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at the State University of New York at Binghamton. My research interests are Applied Microeconomics, Production Econometrics, and Industrial Organization. I study how technology adoption changes firm productivity, input choices, and market power, using firm-level data and structural econometric models.

My research asks why technology investments generate uneven productivity gains and whether improved firm performance reflects greater efficiency or greater pricing power. I develop methods that account for firms’ input decisions, with applications to manufacturing and banking. My agenda also addresses causal mechanisms and reliable productivity measurement.

In teaching, I connect intuitive explanations with formal methods and practical applications. I taught Statistical Methods in Summer 2025 and lead R instruction in Introduction to Econometrics. I help students explain their reasoning, interpret results, and develop independent understanding. Read about my teaching experience and approach.
