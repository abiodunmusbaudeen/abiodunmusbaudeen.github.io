Teaching

I enjoy helping students make sense of ideas they find difficult. Through one-on-one and small-group support, I encourage students to question assumptions, explain their reasoning, and apply economics to real-life situations. My standard for understanding is that students can explain a concept in their own words, work through its reasoning, and interpret the result.

At Binghamton University, I taught Statistical Methods in Summer 2025. My teaching assistantships span introductory and intermediate microeconomics, Principles of Macroeconomics, econometrics, and data analytics. In Introduction to Econometrics, I also lead R instruction.

Teaching Approach and Evidence

Build understanding before moving to applications

In Introduction to Econometrics, students struggled to derive ordinary least squares estimators and prove unbiasedness and consistency. I returned to the foundations, deriving the estimators through least squares and the method of moments and explaining the assumptions behind each proof. Students explained the reasoning back to me and derived the estimators in class. Their explanations and derivations helped me assess when they were ready for policy applications and hypothesis testing. I also organized extra classes and additional office hours.

Connect concepts, computation, and interpretation

In microeconomics, I ask students to predict the effects of a tax, work through the graph, and explain the result in words. In R instruction, I connect data preparation, estimation, and interpretation through practical examples and reproducible scripts. Students learn to explain why a method fits a question, which assumptions it requires, and where its conclusions are limited.

Provide practice and support beyond class

I assigned weekly homework and additional practice questions, created online lecture videos, and shared slides and lecture notes so students could revisit difficult steps at their own pace. An online collaboration platform let students discuss concepts with classmates before bringing unresolved questions to me. Flexible office hours complemented this space with individual support. I encourage questions and treat mistakes as starting points for examining reasoning and learning.

Connect classroom reasoning to practical decisions

My work at First Bank of Nigeria and Konga required making quantitative analysis useful to others. This experience informs my emphasis on clear explanations and documentation that another person can follow. My Nigerian banking paper, published in Economic Modelling (2026), offers an application connecting theory, estimation, and interpretation. Its public replication code provides material for future reproducibility exercises.
